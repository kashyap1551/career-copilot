# Decisions

One line per decision: date, decision, reason.

- 2026-11-13: Scope is anyone with a CV and a target job, not only international students.
- 2026-11-13: A 10-day sprint to a live MVP first, optimised afterwards. Reason: a long plan risks being dropped.
- 2026-11-13: Fit levels, gaps and counts come from rules in code, never from the AI. Reason: the same input always gives the same answer, and every answer can be tested.
- 2026-11-13: Groq's free tier for the AI, in testing and live, starting with openai/gpt-oss-120b. Reason: €0, strict JSON-schema output, and tests run on the model we ship. Not chosen: Gemini's free tier (its terms require paid services for apps offered to users in the EEA) and Mistral's free plan (its requests may be used for training).
- 2026-11-13: The MVP keeps each person's data in their own browser; accounts and a database come after the MVP. Reason: faster to build, and no personal data stored on a server.
- 2026-11-13: Instructions hidden inside a pasted ad can't be fully prevented. The output only affects the person who pasted it. We guard against invented facts by checking quotes against the ad.
- 2026-11-14: An ad's language wording maps to a level once, here: a stated level (B2, "C1-Niveau") is used as written; native, mother tongue or "Muttersprache" is C2; fluent, business fluent, "fließend" or "verhandlungssicher" is C1; good, advanced or "gut" is B2; conversational or intermediate is B1; basic or "Grundkenntnisse" is A2; a language named with no level wording is null (not stated). Reason: the same words always give the same level, and "not stated" beats a guess. A first version; change it here and in the tests together.
- 2026-11-14: Eligibility conditions code can't judge (education level, graduation year, work permit) are kept as exact quotes in `otherConditions` on the job, listed in the report as "check this yourself" and never scored. Reason: scoring them would be a guess, and a wrong score on eligibility is worse than none.
- 2026-11-14: Exception to rule 5 (public ads only): the 3 job ads in fixtures/jobs.ts are synthetic samples written by Claude at the owner's request, each marked source "synthetic sample". Reason: the rules engine only needs realistic shapes, and Day 2 shouldn't wait on ad hunting. Real public ads replace them before the reading test (step 3.2).
- 2026-11-14: A requirement with 'or' is one requirement with several acceptable skills; any one counts as met, and in the unlock ranking, gaining any one of them unlocks the job. Reason: "Power BI or Tableau" is one ask, so counting it as two would make a job look harder or easier than it is.
- 2026-11-14: One line of an ad is one requirement unless it lists separate skills with 'and' or commas; 'or' means alternatives. Reason: the same wording always splits the same way, so counts don't depend on who typed the ad.
- 2026-11-14: Years in an ad map to minYears and are compared with the person's total years; the report says so. Only the lower bound is stored ("2 to 4 years" is 2). A years line adds a skill only if its field is a distinct skill named nowhere else in the ad. Reason: a CV rarely gives years per skill, and being over-experienced is not a mismatch.
- 2026-11-14: Fit level counts gaps = missing must-haves + conflicts that can be closed. Any conflict that can't be closed soon is Long shot. Otherwise: 0 gaps is Strong; exactly one missing must-have and no conflict is Good; one closable conflict alone, or 2 gaps, is Stretch; 3 or more gaps is Long shot. Example: two missing must-haves plus a closable language gap is 3 gaps, so Long shot, even though two missing alone would be Stretch. Reason: it extends the written rules to mixes they don't mention, with one counting rule instead of special cases. Nice-to-haves never change the level.
- 2026-11-14: A conflict is closable if the person is one year short on total years or available up to 30 days after the start date (both set in lib/engine/rules.ts), or one language level short. Two or more levels, years or more than 30 days short, or a missing language, can't be closed soon. Reason: simple thresholds anyone can check by hand; tune them as real ads come in.
- 2026-11-14: A hybrid job counts as on-site for the city check: if the person's cities don't include the job's city, it can't be closed soon. Reason: "three days a week in the office" still means being there.
- 2026-11-14: "Not stated" is never a conflict: a language with no level (person has it at any level), a job with no city, work mode or start date, and years or availability the person didn't give are listed as "not judged". Reason: rule 3, "not stated" beats a guess.
- 2026-11-14: A job counts as unlocked only if it becomes Strong by gaining skills. Any conflict, closable or not, blocks it (a closable conflict is still a gap, so skills alone can't make the job Strong). Blocked jobs are reported with their conflict kinds and whether they could all be closed, never silently dropped; jobs already Strong are never counted as unlocked; nice-to-haves play no part. Reason: "N more Strong matches" must be a number the person can reach by learning alone.
- 2026-11-14: The skill ranking is greedy and cumulative: take the skill that unlocks the most jobs, assume it is gained, repeat. Ties go to the skill that brings the most jobs one step closer, then to skills already in progress (earliest expected date first), then to the skill id. Greedy gives a clear, repeatable order, not a guaranteed best one. Reason: the same input always gives the same order, and nobody is told to start Power BI while already learning Tableau.
- 2026-11-14: A job that needs two missing skills is never counted as unlocked by one of them. It counts as "one step closer" for each skill that covers one of its missing must-haves, used only as the second tie-break and shown as a count. Of several alternatives ("Power BI or Tableau"), gaining any one unlocks the job and the others drop out of the ranking. Reason: honest counts, and the ranking never stalls on jobs that need two skills.
- 2026-11-14: The dated timeline is cumulative: at each date the person is assumed to have every in-progress skill dated on or before it, so skills sharing a date are added together and a job needing both counts at that date. Dates that unlock nothing stay in the list with 0, and the engine does not filter past dates because it does not know today's date. Reason: the count never depends on the order of skills, and "this skill doesn't help yet" is worth showing.
- 2026-11-14: A skill is identified by an id, never free text, in every engine type; constraints are stored apart from skills; a skill the person added with no CV line is never counted as met. Reason: counts must be exact, and every "met" needs a CV line.
